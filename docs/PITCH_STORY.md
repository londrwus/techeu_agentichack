# Orbit: story-led 5-minute pitch

Two people carry the story from start to finish:
- **Maya** runs purchasing for a chain of 40 cafés in London. She buys coffee, chocolate, olive oil and laptops for her staff, and she signs leases on new sites. *(the business)*
- **Leo** is a student in Peckham who's saving for a new graphics card. *(the consumer)*

About 5 minutes. Lines in *(italics)* are optional; cut them if you're running long.

---

### 1 · Landing: the problem (0:00–0:40)
> "Meet Maya. She buys coffee, chocolate and olive oil for forty London cafés. Last year her coffee bill went up by almost half, and nobody warned her. The drought that caused it was visible **from space months earlier**, over Brazil's coffee farms.
> Now meet Leo. He's been saving for a graphics card, and its price keeps climbing, because AI data centres in Texas are buying every chip and every memory module they can.
> Both of them found out at the till. Traders pay millions for early warning like this; Maya and Leo get nothing.
> So we built **Orbit: satellite intelligence for commodities, fully agentic.** **Google Gemini** sees, **Jev** judges, and **Modal** runs it at scale."

### 2 · Earth: where prices are born (0:40–1:05)
> "This is what Orbit watches: twenty places, in real **Sentinel-2** satellite images. The coffee farms Maya depends on in Brazil, Vietnam and Colombia. Cocoa in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana. Olive groves in Spain.
> And for Leo, the AI data centres and chip factories that decide what his GPU will cost.
> *(Every orange ring here is a place where something is going wrong on the ground right now.)*"

### 3 · Overview: Maya's Monday morning (1:05–1:25)
> "This is Maya's Monday-morning screen. Every market she buys in has a single pressure score, a forecast, and the chance it rises.
> Her basket of goods is heading **up about 3 % in six months**. Across forty cafés that's real money, and now she knows **before** the supplier calls her."

### 4 · Groceries: stock up or wait (1:25–1:50)
> "We made it feel like shopping, because that's how everyone thinks about prices.
> Each product gets a plain call: **stock up, hold or wait**. Maya clicks one and sees its price history, the farm it comes from photographed from orbit, and the exact news stories that moved the forecast.
> *(This is the difference between 'prices are going up' and 'here's why, and here's the evidence'.)*"

### 5 · GPU & Gadgets: Leo's question (1:50–2:35)
> "Now Leo's question: **should I buy my GPU now or wait?**
> This is the Stargate AI campus in Abilene, Texas. **Drag the slider**: that was empty land in 2019, and look at it today. You're watching AI demand being built, from space.
> Orbit breaks the price down in pounds, so anyone can follow it: AI data centres buying chips, a global memory shortage, new data centres we measure by satellite. Even the reservoir that cools Taiwan's chip factories is **73 % below normal**.
> The answer: **£1,650 today, about £1,758 by February, a 66 % chance it goes up.** Leo buys this month. And Maya's IT team orders the laptops now, not next quarter."

### 6 · Rent Radar: the biggest bill (2:35–3:00)
> "Maya's biggest cost isn't coffee, it's **rent**, and it's Leo's too.
> Orbit measures new construction from orbit across **2,400 neighbourhoods**. Where building slows but demand stays high, rents rise.
> Leo's area, **Peckham: £2,475 a month today, about £2,610 next year.** Leo knows to lock in his contract now. Maya knows which area to open her next café in before the rents catch up."

### 7 · News hub: Jev reads the world (3:00–3:30)
> "Satellites show the ground, but prices also move on **news**. No person can read it all, so watch this.
> *(click Scan)* Orbit is pulling **four thousand fresh headlines** right now, and **Jev** judges every one: is it relevant, which commodity, does the price go up or down, how serious, with **calibrated probabilities**.
> **Sixteen thousand judgments in about six seconds**, on fifty Modal containers, for **under eight cents**. That's Maya's entire research team, done before she finishes her coffee."

### 8 · Mission Control: the engine (3:30–4:00)
> "This is the engine under the hood, running live. Modal spins up to **a hundred containers**, grabs the newest satellite image for every region, and measures crops, water and construction. Jev judges the news, **Google's TimesFM** forecasts on **NVIDIA L4 GPUs**, and **Gemini** explains it.
> No analysts and no monthly report. It runs in under a minute, costs cents, and it works like an **operating system for prices**."

### 9 · Ask Orbit: just ask (4:00–4:25)
> "And Maya doesn't need to learn a dashboard. She just asks.
> *(send: 'Should I buy a GPU now or wait?')* Jev works out what she's asking and how confident it is. Then a **Gemini agent** calls Orbit's own tools (forecasts, satellites, news) and answers in plain English. It's live, not scripted."

### 10 · Track record + business (4:25–5:00)
> "Can you trust it? We tested it honestly on **266 forecasts it had never seen**, from 2023 to today. It gets the direction right **65 % of the time**, beats the 'price stays the same' baseline, and caught about **7 in 10 big price jumps**. We even show the models that lost.
> For businesses like Maya's, Orbit is an **early-warning system for procurement, supply-chain and risk teams**, sold as a subscription dashboard and API. For people like Leo, it's simply knowing when to buy.
> **Orbit: know tomorrow's prices today.** Thank you."
